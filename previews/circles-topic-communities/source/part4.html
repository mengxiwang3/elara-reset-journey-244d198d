Design rationale

Value before commitment

  • Reading is the first reward. Home leads with a short, finite list of useful questions and the best reply to each, not a feed. A woman gets value in her first minute without posting.
  • One contribution is enough. Unanswered questions are one tap away, and replying has its own bar. Asking is a persistent, plain action.
  • The return hook is a person. “Valeria replied to your question” sits at the top of Home, and the thread opens at the new reply. No streaks, no weekly obligation.

Questions, not people-ranking

  • No karma. “This helped me” is private to the author (“Helped 6 women · only you see this”). It quietly orders replies and the “Helpful this month” view; no one sees a score.
  • Topics, not dozens of categories. Six topics, each with a stated purpose. Rooms are separate, optional, and capped at ten.
  • Elara stays in her lane. She suggests similar questions and asks a room's questions. She never answers in a thread in a woman's voice.

Audience is never a guess

  • Every composer states who sees the post: “Women in Elara Circles, with your first name”, or without your name. Private Ask Elara and Clarity content is never included unless she brings in a specific line and sees exactly what will be posted.
  • Rooms say “Private · N women · M seats open” on every room screen.
  • Report, hide, block and mute sit behind the “⋯” on every post and reply, in two taps, in plain words.

Grounding in the current app. I read the Circles screens and strings in apps/mobile, the Circles wire contract in packages/shared/src/circles.ts, the theme tokens and docs/CIRCLE.md. Today the app has a ten-seat “hall” of rooms and, behind a server flag, a beta feed with “My conversations”, a post menu (report, hide, delete), block, and a “Circles aren't monitored overnight” card. The topic names start from the six Clarity areas, plus one the app suggests: Mothers Circle and Family Money Talks already exist as rooms, so “Motherhood & Family” is a topic without a Clarity area behind it. “Energy & Daily Life” merges the Energy and Everyday areas. What to test with real women before implementation

Does reading pay off?

  • First visit, no account history: do they find a useful question and reply in under two minutes, without being told where to look?
  • Do they understand that Circles is for questions, and that a room is something else?

Will she contribute once?

  • Of women who read, how many ask or reply, and what stops the rest (fear of being seen, not knowing what's allowed, nothing to add)?
  • Do the audience lines change what she is willing to write? Test “with my name” against “without my name”.

Does a reply bring her back?

  • Which notification wording brings her back without making her feel watched? Test lock-screen text with and without the reply preview.
  • After a reply, does she thank, reply again or leave? Does “This helped me” feel natural, or a little odd?

Is it safe and fair?

  • Do women find report, hide and block, and trust that hiding tells nobody? Test with a deliberately unkind reply.
  • Do women read a private room as a promotion, or as relief? What makes them accept an invitation, and what does a quiet room feel like to those in it?

To verify before real-member testing

  • Audience enforced on the server. A post meant for a room must never be readable by women outside it. Topic posts need an explicit visibility field. Ask Elara and Clarity content must never be included in a post unless she brings in a specific line. This prototype shows the promise and proves none of it.
  • Anonymous posting. The server must keep authorship for moderation while never returning it to other members, including in replies, notifications and search.
  • Moderation capacity at 80,000. The current app says reports are read “during the day”. A public topic space needs a queue, response targets, a self-harm path and trained hosts. Nothing here assumes they exist.
  • Access. Circles is currently part of Premium. Open topics could be free to read or require membership; that is a business decision this design does not make.
  • Notifications. The “one a day” digest and lock-screen wording shown here are proposals, not what the app does today.
