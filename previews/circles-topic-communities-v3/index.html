Elara Circles · life areas and life stages · v3 · design prototype, not in the app

Read first. Find your stage. Join to ask or reply.

Women join several Circles, based on what they care about or are going through. A Circle is an open topic community; a room is an optional, private group. Click through the phone, or jump to a screen. Four paths are marked: a new woman choosing Circles, a returning woman reading and replying, a woman joining another Circle later, and a busy Circle at scale. Anyone in Elara can read a Circle; you join that Circle to ask or reply in it (a proposal to test).

Design rationale

Two kinds of Circle, named plainly

  • Life areas (what she cares about) and Life stages (what she is going through) are separate groups everywhere they appear: Explore, search results, the destination picker.
  • On Explore, Life areas and Life stages are two tabs at the top, always visible, so a stage is one tap away instead of below six areas.
  • Six areas and five stages start as a curated set that can sustain conversation. They are proposed names, not a launch list. Narrower interests are tags until enough women are talking to support a Circle.
  • A Circle is open and topic-led. A room is a private group of up to ten women, optional, and kept out of the main navigation.

Useful before a post

  • Home leads with one useful question and its best reply. A new woman can read it before she has joined anything; her “Your Circles” invitation is one quiet line below it, not a boxed empty state.
  • The date and the large “What's on your mind?” box stay gone. Ask is a compact, always-visible pill.
  • Search covers Circle names, questions and tags, with an Unanswered filter. Unanswered is also one tap on Home.

Clear destinations and a calm path

  • Every question row names its Circle. Before she posts, the composer shows where it will appear, who can read it and who can reply. She must choose.
  • Browsing is open. Joining is one tap, adds the Circle to Your Circles, and is private to her by default.
  • With several Circles joined, a switcher and an “All your Circles” list keep navigation clear. No red unread badges; the cues are words.

Who can read, and who must join

One rule, said the same way everywhere

  • Read: any woman in Elara can read any Circle's questions and replies, joined or not.
  • Ask or reply: only members of that Circle. Joining is one tap.
  • Which Circles she joined: private to her. Her first name still appears on what she writes.

Where it is said

  • Explore: a one-line rule under the tabs.
  • Circle preview, not joined: “You can read everything here. Join … to ask or reply.”
  • Thread: “Any woman in Elara can read this”, “Asked in …”; reply bar: “Any woman in Elara can read your reply”.
  • Ask: separate lines for Who can read it and Who can reply.

What v2 got wrong

  • “Seen by women in Elara Circles” could mean members of this Circle, or members of any Circle.
  • “Join to get these questions … and to ask or reply” joined two different ideas in one sentence.
  • This is a proposal. Whether non-members can read, and whether it should differ for sensitive stages, still needs a decision and testing.

At scale: proposed ways to surface an unanswered question

These are design proposals to test. None of them is built. The app has no question routing, no host queue, no “For you” ordering and no response-time promise today, and the screens promise no reply time.

Rules

  • Waiting for a first reply sits at the top of a busy Circle: the oldest few, with how long each has waited.
  • A question leaves that list after its first reply. It stays in the Circle's New list.
  • Members can follow tags. Waiting questions with a followed tag are marked “For you” and sorted first. What she follows is visible only to her.
  • Home shows at most three at a time under “Could use your reply”, each with the reason it is there. No red badges; no countdown.

Fairness and care

  • A woman's first question in a Circle could be surfaced to members first, as a welcome (to test).
  • A question she skips should not return the next day (to test).
  • Matching uses only what she joined and the tags she chose to follow. Never her private Ask Elara chats, Clarity or Path content.
  • Sensitive stages (Pregnancy & New Parenthood, Divorce & Separation, Caregiving) need trained hosts and a safety path before this ships. See “To verify”.

What to learn

  • Do relevant members find the question, or does “waiting” feel like homework?
  • Is three a day right? Does “For you” feel helpful or like being watched?
  • Do first replies come sooner, and are they good? Which questions still go unanswered?

What to test with real women before implementation

Choosing Circles

  • Do women understand “Life areas” versus “Life stages” without explanation? Do they join one of each, or several of one kind?
  • Do they browse before joining? Do they read a Circle's purpose and sample questions, or just join?
  • Which situations have no home, and which tags do they reach for?

Reading, asking, replying

  • Does the first screen give useful content to a woman with no Circles, and to one with five?
  • Is the destination of a new question clear before posting? Do women pick the right Circle, or the first one?
  • What does “join to reply” do to a woman who wanted to reply once?

Navigation at scale

  • With three, six and ten joined Circles, can she find a question she read yesterday?
  • Do unanswered cues bring women to answer, or feel like a to-do list? Does “For you” help or feel like being watched?
  • With 38 new questions a day, does the unanswered question reach someone who can help?
  • Do rooms stay optional, or are they mistaken for another kind of Circle?

To verify before real-member testing

  • Membership privacy and audience enforcement. Joined Circles must not be visible to other members. Posts need an explicit audience. Private Ask Elara and Clarity content must never be posted unless she brings in a specific line.
  • Moderation capacity for open Circles at 80,000. Sensitive life stages (Divorce & Separation, Pregnancy & New Parenthood, Caregiving) need trained hosts, a self-harm path and a response target. The app today says reports are read “during the day”.
  • Search and tags. Search must respect blocks, hidden posts and anonymous authorship, and must not surface private rooms.
  • Access. Circles is currently part of Premium. Open reading and joining could be free or paid; this design does not decide it.
  • Surfacing rules at scale (waiting list, followed tags, “For you”, three at a time, first-question welcome) are proposals; nothing here is built, and no response time is promised.
  • Proposals, not facts: the Circle names, “read freely, join to ask or reply”, activity labels, and the tags-to-Circle rule.
