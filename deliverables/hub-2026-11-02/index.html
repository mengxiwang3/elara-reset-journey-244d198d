How Elara should reply each day

Today Elara is told to «reflect back» what she wrote, so her daily reply mostly praises and mirrors. We compared the current prompt (V0) with three new versions (V1–V3) on 56 answers written to resemble what testers might say; there are no real user answers yet. Eight separate Claude reviewer agents scored every reply twice without knowing which version wrote it. They are the same kind of model that wrote the replies, so their agreement (96–100%) overstates how certain this is.

Full results

The seven Money sessions, fixed

  • Support path moves forward: she names what to clarify, then where she could ask (Elara never invents a local service), then gets a two-sentence message she can copy.
  • Session 5 fits every path: a job filter, the conditions of a procedure or support, or a first test for a business idea.
  • Session 7 no longer assumes a job offer.

Three A/B pairs

  1. Keep session 2, or fold it into session 1 when she already chose «Cubrir algo básico».
  2. No recap, or open sessions 2–7 with «Ayer te llevaste: …».
  3. Elara replies after every step, or once at the end.

480 automated layout and logic checks pass (every path and variant, four screen sizes, larger text). This shows the screens work. It doesn't show they're useful, and they haven't been tried on real phones or with screen readers. Checked in a browser

Kit for the test round

  • Schedule: 4 or 8 women over 7 days, so every A/B pair splits evenly (6 leaves session 2 at 2 vs 4). Live sessions on days 1, 4 and 7; about 1.5 hours of facilitator time per tester.
  • Each tester gets a code (#t1, #t2…) that fixes her variants. Notes stay in her browser; nothing is tracked.
  • Script in Spanish with three questions that matter: what is this asking and what would it help you with; what in the reply came from what you wrote; what did you remember, use or try.
  • Concept check, 6–8 minutes: would she use job opportunities and matching with other women? It starts from what she actually did last time and ends with a forced choice, because «sí, lo usaría» alone is weak evidence.
