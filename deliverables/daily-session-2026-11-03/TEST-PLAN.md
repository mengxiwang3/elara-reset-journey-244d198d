# Usability test: could this daily-session design be wrong?

The aim is to **disprove** the preferred design: purpose first, a visible link between the two questions, a takeaway she can find again, and respectful skips. Each claim has a result, written down in advance, that would count against it. With 6 people this is qualitative: report every number as «n de N» and keep quotes verbatim.

## Setup

- **People:** 6 Spanish-speaking women from Elara's audience with varied money situations (work, irregular income, caregiving, no current income). They need consent and a clear right to stop. They may invent answers, and nobody has to share real figures.
- **Build:** the redesigned day 8, Money path, Wuzhen. Before the test, replace the simulated replies with **real production-model replies**. If that isn't possible, drop the feedback questions (step 5) rather than judge simulated text.
- **Comparison:**
  - 3 people use the current app's day 8 (control).
  - 3 people use the redesign.
  - At the end, everyone briefly sees the conversation prototype (concept B) as a challenger.
  - Nobody is told which version they have.
- **Facilitator:** someone who didn't write the copy. Fixed wording, no explaining the screen, think-aloud, screen recording.

## Session (about 25 minutes), then a next-day check (5 minutes)

1. **Opening.** Let her read, then before «Empezar» ask: «Con tus palabras, ¿para qué es la práctica de hoy?» Record the time on screen.
2. **Q1.** Let her choose freely and note any hesitation. Give two people a scenario card that leads to «Ninguna por ahora» and one person a card that leads to «Prefiero pasar».
3. **Q2.** Before she writes, ask: «¿Qué te pide esta pregunta?» Record the time to first keystroke, and whether her answer is a concrete question or step, vague, or blank.
4. **Change.** Halfway through Q2: «Imagina que quieres cambiar tu elección.» Watch whether she finds «Cambiar», whether her text survives, and what she makes of the note.
5. **Feedback** (real replies only):
   - «¿Qué de la nota de Elara salió de lo que escribiste?»
   - «¿Algo de la nota suponía cosas sobre ti que no dijiste?»
6. **Closing:** «¿Qué te llevas de hoy?» This is the brief's success question.
7. **Next day, remote:**
   - Without the app open: «¿Qué anotaste ayer?»
   - Then: «Abre la app y muéstrame dónde lo encontrarías.» Record the time and whether she needed help.
8. **Challenger.** Show concept B for 2 minutes: «¿Cuál te ayudaría más a sentir que Elara te escuchó? ¿Por qué?»

## What would disprove the design (decided before testing)

| Claim | Disproved if… |
|---|---|
| The opening makes the day's purpose clear | 2 or more of the 3 redesign users can't paraphrase the purpose, **or** most spend under 2 s on the opening (unread). Then drop it or fold the purpose into Q1. |
| The choice row and the new instruction fix the Q1→Q2 link | 2 or more of 3 still ask «¿sobre qué?» or describe Q2 as unrelated to their choice. |
| The takeaway is useful and findable | 2 or more of 3 can't say what they took away (step 6), **or** can't find it from Hoy the next day without help within about 10 s. |
| The flow isn't heavier | The redesign takes clearly longer (about 1.5× the control) without better results on the three rows above. |
| Feedback is grounded | Any participant points to an assumption about her life the note made, or 2 or more call it generic. |
| Skips and «Ninguna» are respected | Anyone on those paths says she felt judged, pushed to act, or that something was put in her mouth. |
| Step-by-step beats a conversation | Most people prefer B with concrete reasons about feeling heard. Then revisit B once reply quality is proven. |

The control matters: if the control group does as well on purpose and the Q1→Q2 link, the redesign hasn't earned its extra screen.

## What not to conclude

- Completing the day, liking it, coming back and doing something in real life are four different signals. Report them separately.
- Six people can show problems; they can't prove retention or effectiveness.
- A preference for one version isn't evidence that it helps more.
