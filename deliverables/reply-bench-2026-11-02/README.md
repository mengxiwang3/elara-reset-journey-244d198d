# Daily-reply test bench (2 Nov 2026)

This bench asks one question: which instructions should Elara's daily reply get, so that it gives her something useful instead of mirroring her answer?

> **Simulated with Claude, not the production model or settings; directional evidence only.**
>
> The app sends replies to `openai/gpt-5.6-luna` (`CHAT_MODEL` in `apps/api/wrangler.toml`), with reasoning effort `low` and at most 400 output tokens, through `askModel` in `apps/api/src/lib/ai.ts`. The fallback is `@cf/openai/gpt-oss-120b`. This bench did not call that model.

## Method

1. **Current prompt (V0).** Copied exactly from `apps/api/src/services/stepReply.ts` and `services/chat.ts` at app commit `1363407` (30 Oct):
   - `BASE_PROMPT`;
   - the Sage persona (the default);
   - `jobFor`;
   - the format and rules block;
   - the Spanish language prompt.

   The user message uses the app's format: «The question they were asked…», then «Under it…», then «What they answered: …», with `describeAnswer` labels such as `(they wrote)`, `(they skipped this one)` and `(none of the options; in their own words)`.
2. **Variants.** The full prompts are in `variants/`, with an example for session 4 (mid-day step) and session 7 (last step).
   - **V1:** the handoff proposal. It adds the day's purpose, the takeaway and step guidance, and asks for one useful move with no praise, interpretation or new task. On the last step it names her takeaway.
   - **V2:** V1, plus an explicit silent choice of one move type (distinction, tighter draft, the fact and who confirms it, or a feasible next step). It also says how to handle skips, «no puedo», unsure answers and corrections.
   - **V3:** V1, but the last step may end with one optional short question.
   - **All variants:** the persona, the safety rules and the language prompt are identical across V0–V3.
3. **Cases.** `cases.json` has 138 realistic Spanish answers across the seven test sessions (about 20 each). They cover the three paths (empleo, negocio, apoyo) and these answer types:
   - contrasting pairs;
   - vague and very short answers;
   - «hoy no puedo»;
   - corrections;
   - off-topic and mixed Spanish and English;
   - answers that mention another person;
   - a scam-like job offer;
   - a support-path answer to a job-shaped question.

   There is no crisis content. In the app, safety screening runs before the reply, so it's out of scope here.
4. **Question source.** For sessions 1, 2, 4 and 5 the bench uses the app's real question text, from the 210-day draft in `tools/curriculum/curriculum-210.json` (MONEY-W1-D1, W1-D4, W2-D2, W2-D3). For sessions 3, 6 and 7 the app asks different questions under the same IDs, so the bench uses the handoff's wording. Each session's `src_app` field in `sessions.json` records which.
5. **Generation.** 56 cases (8 per session, flagged `sample: true`), times 4 variants, gives 224 replies. All are in `outputs.jsonl`.
6. **Scoring** (`scores.jsonl`):
   - **Manual judge:** the bench author scored (a) one useful move, or correct acceptance of a skip, «no puedo» or stop; (b) praise; (c) unsupported interpretation; (d) new task; and (g) natural Spanish.
   - **Automatic judge:** a second, rule-based check scored (b) praise, (e) uses her words, (f) length (3 sentences or fewer, 280 characters or fewer) and whether the reply ends on a question to her.
   - **Contrast test:** 8 groups of contrasting answers (7 pairs and one group of three), judged for whether the replies differ in substance.

## Limits

- **Simulated generation.** Replies were simulated by Claude, not generated by the production model. GPT-5.6-luna at effort `low` may follow these instructions differently, especially the conflicting question rules in V0.
- **No independent sampling.** The intended design used separate, blind generator and judge subagents. That wasn't available, so one Claude instance wrote every variant's replies and also did the manual scoring. It knew which variant was which, so treat the gap between V0 and the others as an upper bound.
- **Small sample.** The 56 scored cases are a stratified sample. The other 82 cases are ready for a real run.
- **Rough agreement figure.** The only agreement measure is manual against rule-based on praise (95–98%). Rule-based checks are crude.
- **No users involved.** Whether women find the replies useful still needs the tester round.

## How to rerun on the real model

`run-inputs.json` holds all 552 request pairs (138 cases × 4 variants), each with a `system` prompt and a `user` message in the app's format. A developer with an API key can send them to `openai/gpt-5.6-luna` with effort `low` and 400 maximum tokens, then score the results with the same rubric. The best setup is two blind human judges, María and one other. Compare against `summary.json`.
