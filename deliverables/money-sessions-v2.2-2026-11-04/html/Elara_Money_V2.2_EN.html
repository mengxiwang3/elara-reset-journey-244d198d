SHARED BEHAVIOUR

One learning point, one rule, one connection

An editorial proposal. Page 20 says which parts the app already supports.

Five screens per visit

1. Opening: one question, up to five options, “Other” and “Prefer to skip”. 2. Listen or read: 35-45 s audio with at most three ideas, three cards and “Read instead”. 3. Today’s rule

Every session leaves one short rule, stated clearly. Caveats stay, but at most one per screen: several in a row make it feel as if nothing was learned.

When to go deeper

Optional button: “Think a little further.” It opens at most one question from the branch matching her choice. Her answer changes the draft or the saved detail. If no branch fits, offer continuing or an example without assigning an emotion.

What Elara says

Every reply is written in advance; none is generated by AI. If she picks “Other” and writes, Elara doesn’t interpret her words: it uses the line written for “Other” in that session and continues. That keeps the generic note from coming back.

Learning at her own pace

“Prefer to skip” and “Use an example” are always available. After skipping an activity, offer the explanation or continue. An omission never becomes an answer or an error.

Memory she can correct

Saving is optional. Recover only what she saved, with “Edit” and “Remove”. In session 7, omit missing fields. Never invent a transformation.

Privacy and honest states

Do not share answers with María or Circles by default. Do not record personal audio: the audio is teaching content. The close recognises what she did; it does not certify mastery.

Cases and numbers

Fictional examples in dollars ($), as a proposal. Calculations live in code, with dates, rates and assumptions visible. No product or allocation recommendations, account connections, sending drafts or moving money within the sessions.

Closing without an activity: “The explanation is here if you want to come back.” Closing with a card: show only what she chose to keep. No option schedules a reminder on its own.

2 / Listen or read (complete script)

Imagine two pots of money: one for a move next month and one you won’t touch for years. Saving means keeping money aside so it’s available. Investing means putting it to work so it can grow, accepting that it can also fall, even right when you need it. So the first question isn’t how much it could earn, but what it’s for and when you’ll need it. You don’t need money today to learn this.

Years to wait

First question

3 / Check an idea

Paula has to pay for a move in four weeks. She’s offered an investment that can rise or fall. What would you check first?

A. How much she might earn if all goes well.
It’s usually the first thing shown, but it doesn’t tell her whether she’ll have what she needs in four weeks. If it falls just before, she’s short for the move.
B. Whether she’ll have the money on time, even if it falls. [Supported idea]
Yes. Paula already knows when she needs it: in four weeks. With so little time, what matters most is that the money is there.
C. Whether it went well for other people.
What happened to others doesn’t say what will happen in the next four weeks, and doesn’t change Paula’s date.

05. No magic: change a number and look

See how a gain, a debt or rising prices enter the next calculation, and why time changes everything.

3-5 min proposed / Demonstration (v1: cards revealed on choice) + voiceover / estimated narration: 40-50 s

1 / Opening

When other people talk about investing, what comes up for you?
CuriosityI feel lateI distrust what they showNothing in particular

“Other” with free text · “Prefer to skip”

If she picks “Other” and writes, Elara replies: “Thanks. Let’s look at a simple calculation; you choose what to change.”

2 / Listen or read (complete script)

$100 at 5% a year becomes $105 after one year. If that gain stays, next year the 5% is calculated on $105, not $100. At first you barely notice; over time you do: after 20 years it would be about $265, against $200 if the gain didn’t stay. And it works the same way against you, with a debt or with rising prices. The 5% is an assumption, not a promise: a real investment can also fall.

Hypothetical base
$100 at 5% a year, 20 years: $265. If the gain didn’t stay: $200.
More time
30 years: $432.
Against you
Prices rising 10% a year: what costs $100 today costs $121 in 2 years.

3 / Explore what changes

Choose an assumption to change. The calculation and an explanation appear; no right guess is needed first.

A. Ten more years (30 in total).
100 × 1.05³⁰ ≈ $432. The last ten years add more than the first twenty together, because the gain grows on earlier gains. Still assumes 5% every year and no added money.
B. Two years with a 10% fall.
100 × 0.90² = $81. Value can also fall. The example doesn’t predict a particular loss.
C. Prices rising 10% a year.
100 × 1.10² = $121. What costs $100 today would cost $121 in two years. If your money doesn’t grow, it buys less. Same calculation, working against you.
D. A $100 debt at 30% a year, unpaid.
100 × 1.30² = $169. If interest is added to the debt, next year you’re charged interest on interest. Simplified example: real cards and loans calculate in other ways.

No winning option: each choice shows its consequence. “Prefer to skip” lets her read the explanation or continue.

06. When an investment promises too much

Recognise three warning signs before sending money, without distrusting everything or blaming anyone.

3-4 min proposed / Audio + fictional message / estimated narration: 35-45 s

1 / Opening

Have you ever been offered something to “grow your money” fast?
Yes, on WhatsApp or social mediaYes, by someone I knowI’ve seen it happen to othersNeverI’ve put money into something like that

“Other” with free text · “Prefer to skip”

If she picks “Other” and writes, Elara replies: “Thanks. Let’s look at three signs that work for any offer.”

2 / Listen or read (complete script)

Every real investment can fall. So if someone guarantees you high returns, they’re promising something they can’t know. Pressure is a sign too: “today only”, “only a few places left”. And an offer coming from someone you trust or from your community doesn’t make it safe: many scams arrive that way, and the person passing it on may truly believe in it.

Guaranteed returns
No real investment can guarantee them.
Decide now
Pressure takes away your time to check.
Bring more people
If you earn by recruiting, ask where the money comes from.

3 / Check an idea

Lucía gets this message (fictional example): “Invest $100 today and in a month you’ll have $150. Guaranteed. 3 places left. Bring two friends and you earn more.” What should she do first?

A. Put in just a little to try it.
Trying it with a little doesn’t protect her: many of these schemes pay out the first time so you trust them and put in more.
B. Ask whether the sender got paid.
Someone being paid doesn’t prove it’s safe: in these schemes, early payouts often come from the money of people who join later.
C. Send nothing until she checks who’s offering it. [Supported idea]
Yes. The message has all three signs: guaranteed returns, pressure and bringing more people. Before sending money, she can check whether whoever is offering it is authorised by her country’s financial regulator.
